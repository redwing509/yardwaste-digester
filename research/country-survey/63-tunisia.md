## Summary

Tunisia has no anaerobic digester for yard or park waste. Cities compost it: Bizerte's green-waste centre (since 2021) composts about 2,000 t a year of park waste, a model copied in Gafsa and Mahdia, and Tunis composts green waste at the Belvédère park station. A national survey estimates about 31,500 t a year of waste from urban green spaces (3 t/ha on more than 10,000 ha). Tunisian digestion research uses food waste, cattle manure, market fruit and vegetable waste and macroalgae; a modelled 30,000 t/yr food-plus-agricultural-waste biogas plant would lose money at the local 8 US cent/kWh power price. The only plant making power from waste gas (Oued Laya, Sousse, 2025) burns landfill gas, not digester gas.

## Verified findings

- **Green-waste quantities and treatment concept** — Chaher et al. (ENIG Gabès, INSAT/University of Carthage, University of Rostock, DBFZ), Sustainability 12(19) 8167, 2020, https://doi.org/10.3390/su12198167: along the coastline "food waste (FW) (102.543 t/a); green waste (GW) (1.326.930 t/a); and cattle manure (CM) (1.548.350 t/a)" (dot = thousands separator); Greater Tunis "10,700 tons/year of gardening residue and around 6085 tons/year of GW" from commercial facilities. Its road map sends 100 % green waste to composting and uses AD only for manure and food waste. Modelled biogas plant: 30 days HRT, "163 m3 of biogas (almost 60% of CH4) per ton of feedstock", 30,000 t/yr (75 % food waste, 25 % agricultural waste), 0.4 MWe, CAPEX about 3.5 million US$; at 8 cent/kWh (STEG) "it is not worth building such a plant"; break-even at 18 cent/kWh. Lab co-digestion best: 0.846 LN/kg VSin.
