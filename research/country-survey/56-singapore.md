## Summary

No Singapore digester running on yard, park or tree waste was found. Singapore's horticultural waste (256,000 t in 2023, 85 % recycled) and wood waste mostly go to biomass power plants and Sembcorp's Energy from Waste plant as fuel (447,000 t of wood + horticultural waste in 2023), not to digesters. The only yard-waste digestion data are lab work at the National University of Singapore (NUS) on campus tree leaves and twigs: pretreating yard waste with digested sludge plus 10 wt % food waste raised methane yield by 35 % (Waste Management 2018), and an NUS AD + gasification hybrid reached 70.8 % overall energy efficiency (Energy 2017). Full-scale and pilot digestion in Singapore is food waste and sewage sludge: PUB's Ulu Pandan co-digestion plant (from 2016), the NUS container digesters at East Coast Lagoon Food Village (about 150 kg/day, 2021) and at a hawker centre (10.4 m³, 0.55 m³ CH₄/kg VS), and the planned Tuas Nexus sludge + food-waste co-digestion. A government-commissioned roadmap lists "food scraps and yard debris" digestion (MBT B) as one screened technology option.

## Verified findings

- **National green-waste stream** — NEA, "Annex B – 2023 Waste and Recycling Statistics" (https://nea.gov.sg/docs/default-source/media-files/news-releases-docs/annex-b_2023-waste-and-recycling-statistics.pdf): 2023 Horticultural 256 ('000 t) generated, 218 recycled, 85 %; Wood 447 generated, 299 recycled, 67 %. "Wood and horticultural waste recycled include 447,000 tonnes sent to facilities (e.g., biomass power plants and Sembcorp's Energy from Waste plant) for use as fuel." Horticultural generation 2019–2022: 400, 313, 332, 221 thousand t.
- **Yard waste + food waste lab digestion (NUS)** — Zhang L., Loh K.-C., Zhang J. (all National University of Singapore), Waste Management 79 (2018) 109–119, doi 10.1016/j.wasman.2018.07.036 (abstract via PubMed 30343737; paywalled): yard waste "biologically pretreated with digested sludge and supplemented with food waste"; optimum 10 wt % food waste, 4-day pretreatment; cellulose reduction +6.5–20.3 %, hemicellulose +14.8–53.1 %; crystallinity down 23 % from 71 %; "methane yield was 35% higher than that of the control digester without FW supplementation". Yard waste = NUS campus tree leaves and twigs. Absolute yields not checked (body not open).
- **AD + gasification hybrid on yard waste (NUS)** — Yao Z., Li W., Kan X. (NUS), Dai Y. (Shanghai Jiao Tong University), Tong Y.W., Wang C.-H. (NUS), Energy 124 (2017) 133–145, doi 10.1016/j.energy.2017.02.035 (abstract via RePEc; paywalled): AD of yard waste mixed with anaerobic sludge, then co-gasification of the AD residue with woody biomass; "Optimal energy efficiency was found to be 70.8% at mixing ratio of 20 wt% AD residue with 30 wt% moisture content." NUS group page: 30 L high-solids and 1000 L semi-continuous digesters; 10 kW, 20 kW and 1 MW gasifiers (https://blog.nus.edu.sg/chbewch/gasification-technology/gasification-technology/ad-gasification/).
- **Food-waste digestion pilots (demarcation: no yard waste)** — NEA–NUS–NParks joint release, 17 Nov 2021 (https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/food-waste-at-east-coast-lagoon-food-village-to-be-turned-into-energy-and-fertiliser-under-pilot-project): NUS container digester at East Coast Lagoon Food Village under NEA's Closing the Waste Loop R&D Initiative; about 60 stalls, "about 150 kg of digestible food waste daily"; biogas engine powers the system, two phone chargers and up to 31 fans; NParks to study digestate for landscaping at East Coast Park. Tiong et al. (NUS), Chemosphere 318 (2023) 137937, doi 10.1016/j.chemosphere.2023.137937: 10.4 m³ digester in two 20-ft containers beside a hawker centre, 71-day start-up, 0.55 ± 0.04 m³ CH₄/kg VS, 56.6 ± 2.3 % methane.
- **Sludge + food-waste co-digestion (demarcation)** — Anaergia reference page (https://www.anaergia.com/reference-facilities/singapore-national-water-agency-pub/): PUB Ulu Pandan Co-Digestion facility, startup 2016, OREX + Omnivore high-solids AD, "13,000 tonnes per day of sewage biosolids 10,000 tonnes per year of source separated food waste", 120 Nm³/h biogas. He X., Tian H., Tong Y.W., Wang C.-H. (NUS), ICAE2020 / Energy Proceedings (2021), doi 10.46855/energy-proceedings-7292 (open PDF): Tuas co-located WRP + WTE design, 400 t/day food waste + 400 t/day sludge at 1:1; "64.3% reduction" in life-cycle GHG and "2129 tonnes CO2-eq reduction potential per year" vs current treatment; biogas fires the WTE superheater. Rajagopal et al. (NTU NEWRI), Sci. Total Environ. 443 (2013) 877–886, doi 10.1016/j.scitotenv.2012.11.016: brown water + food waste 0.54–0.59 L CH₄/g VS added (batch); single-stage SeqBR at 16 d HRT 92 % soluble-COD removal, about 0.41 L CH₄/g VS added.
- **Policy / roadmap** — Solid Waste Management Technology Roadmap (Golder Associates Singapore for NEA, hosted by NCCS; PDF now returns 404, text seen only in a search-index copy): "MBT B – Waste to biogas and fertiliser via AD ... organic materials such as food scraps and yard debris are tipped into an enclosed receiving area ... After two weeks in the hydrolysis percolators, the digestate is removed and composted." One of three screened MBT options, not an adopted plant. For food waste the roadmap's option is co-digestion with used-water sludge.
- **Older Singapore theses (context)** — Lek S.M., NTU dissertation 2004 (hdl 10356/11882): food + horticultural waste were 19.6 % (0.94 million t) of waste collected in 2002; feasibility review of a two-stage anaerobic/aerobic process. Tong Y.W. (NUS), ICOSSE '16 keynote (26 Oct 2016): "a hybrid waste-to-energy system comprising a compact anaerobic digester and a biomass gasification system for converting municipal solid wastes to energy."

## Simulators and modeling tools

Hermes gave no simulators section. Checker found no released digester simulator in Singapore sources. The NUS Chi-Hwa Wang group's "Gasification Process Simulator" (1-D/2-D fixed-bed gasification kinetics; https://blog.nus.edu.sg/chbewch/gasification-technology/gasification-technology/gasification-process-simulator/) is a gasification model with no download or licence, so it is not a digester tool and was not added.
