## Summary

Algeria has no anaerobic digester for yard or park waste. Green waste — about 3 million t a year nationally, "most of which are disposed of unsustainably" — is composted where it is treated at all: the Algiers green-space agency EDEVAL runs nursery composting stations (El Alia, Bouchaoui, Hadjeri/Staoueli), El Khroub (Constantine) has a pruning-waste compost pilot, and sludge–green-waste co-composting is used in the Green Dam reforestation. Since February 2025, Law 25-02 requires organic waste (except waste of animal origin, which has its own rules) to be treated biologically "par compostage et/ou par méthanisation" first, and landfill or treatment sites making biogas must recover its energy; the implementing rules are still to come. Algerian digestion research is lab and pilot scale on household waste, cow and camel dung, kitchen waste and sludge, with an ADM1 model of a 2000 m³ co-digester and a life-cycle study for Algiers.
