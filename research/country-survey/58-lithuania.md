## Summary

Lithuania composts its yard waste; it does not digest it. The national compost network report for 2018 counts 106 640 t of green waste composted but only **1 t** of green waste anaerobically digested (out of 34 793 t digested in total). Green waste has been banned from landfill since 2003, and the authorities steer it to composting (home composting first, then more than 50 green-waste sites). Anaerobic digestion in Lithuania runs at regional MBT plants on the organic fraction of mixed municipal waste and, more recently, on separately collected food waste (Alytus RATC is the largest, about 23 000 t of food waste a year), plus farm plants on manure and crops. No Lithuanian methane yield for yard waste was found.

## Verified findings

- **National numbers (2018)** — ECN Country Report Lithuania, revision 2020-09-15 (https://www.compostnetwork.info/download/country-report-lithuania-2020?filename=ECN-Country-report_Lithuania-updated-2020-v2.pdf&ind=1602501880683&refresh=422ce3d4&wpdmdl=10009): "53 composting sites, 11 MBT plants (4 AD, 6 composting, 1 biodrying), 1 AD outside MBTs that treated biowaste (11 AD have permissions to treat biowaste), 3 incinerators." 391 426 t composted (green waste 106 640 t); 34 793 t anaerobically digested (green waste 1 t; sewage sludge 5 118 t dry matter); 89 399 t green waste collected separately from households. Landfill ban on green waste from 2003. All compost and digestate from MBT used as landfill cover.
- **Green-waste sites** — Aplinkos apsaugos agentūra, 2025-06-17 (https://aaa.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/naujas-interaktyvus-zemelapis-pades-gyventojams-lengviau-rasti-zaliuju-atlieku-surinkimo-aiksteles/): "Šiuo metu visoje Lietuvoje veikia virš 50 žaliųjų atliekų surinkimo aikštelių." Aplinkos apsaugos departamentas, 2026-09-16 (https://aad.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/prasidejus-rudeniui-aplinkosaugininkai-primena-kaip-savo-kieme-tvarkyti-zaliasias-atliekas-Pw3I/): composting is the preferred route for garden waste; sites accept hedge and shrub cuttings, leaves, grass, flowers, weeds, bark, sawdust and chips; burning only by exception.
- **MBT digestion plans (2016)** — EEA/ETC country fact sheet, October 2016 (https://www.eionet.europa.eu/etcs/etc-ce/products/country-profiles-on-the-management-of-municipal-waste-1/lithuania_msw_2016.pdf): four AD facilities in the Alytus, Panevėžys, Telšiai and Utena regions were then "under construction or in the planning phase"; in 2014, 49 green-waste composting sites.
- **Utena MBT (2016 data)** — Kuzmaitė K., MSc thesis, Kaunas University of Technology, 2017 (https://epubl.ktu.edu/object/elaba:22827703): the regional MBT processed over 26 thousand t of municipal waste, including about 10 thousand t of biodegradable waste, with a biogas yield of 24.21 m³/t; separately collected food waste would give about 107.04 m³/t. The thesis says green waste is best handled by home composting.
- **Kaunas MBT scenario** — Abdullah M. (Lithuanian Energy Institute) and Denafas G. (KTU), IOP Conf. Ser. Earth Environ. Sci. 1474 (2025) 012005 (https://doi.org/10.1088/1755-1315/1474/1/012005): Kaunas MBT composts on average 63,474 t/yr of biological waste in tunnels (2018–2022). Replacing this with AD, "72240 tons per year of biodegradable material can generate 87.70 million kWh annually" (a primary-energy estimate; the paper's conclusion contradicts it by calling 87.70 million kWh the plant's demand, and its stated demand elsewhere is 19.27 million kWh/yr).
- **Food-waste digestion (Alytus)** — Dainavos žodis, 2022-06-07 (https://www.dainavoszodis.lt/2022/06/07/kiek-biometano-galima-pagaminti-is-atlieku-atsakymo-ieskota-ir-aratc-patirtyje/): Alytus RATC makes biogas, electricity and compost from biodegradable waste and, as the largest processor, treats about 23 thousand t of food waste a year (Lithuania: about 150 thousand t of food waste a year).
- **Farm biomethane** — EU CAP Network good practice, EIP-AGRI operational group 2022–2024, RDP support 196 925 EUR (https://eu-cap-network.ec.europa.eu/good-practice/turning-farm-waste-renewable-fuel-through-lithuanias-first-practical-biomethane_en): Lithuania's first farm biomethane production, compression and filling system, from manure and agricultural residues, fuelled a tractor, forklift and delivery vehicle. Farm feedstock, not yard waste.
- **Dry-AD pilot (2013)** — ABOWE Report 4.7, Ostfalia University (Germany), October 2014 (https://www.savonia.fi/abowefiles/reports/pilotb/O4.7_Pilot_B_Proof_of_Technology.pdf): a plug-flow dry pilot on a small Lithuanian farm ran on cow manure, distillery leftovers, Klaipėda school/kindergarten food waste and Curonian Spit algae. Foreign team, Lithuanian substrate; no yard waste.
- **Research** — KTU (APINI) on green-waste composting: Kliopova and Stanevičiūtė, EREM 2013 (https://erem.ktu.lt/index.php/erem/article/view/4680). LUT (Finland) + KTU national co-treatment scenarios: Havukainen et al., Waste Manag. Res. 2012 (https://doi.org/10.1177/0734242X11427945; abstract only, numbers paywalled).

## Not taken (unverified, contradicted or not Lithuania)

- "1 500 m³/t methane for food-waste-enriched streams" (Havukainen et al.): not in the abstract and physically impossible.
- "Up to 220 m³/t for source-separated BDW incl. garden waste in Utena": a literature value for food waste quoted in the thesis.
- "Four AD plants operating, wet AD": the 2016 source says under construction or planned and gives no process type.
- "87.70 GWh of electricity": the paper says kWh of energy, not electricity.
- "KTU investigates green-waste AD" (EREM 2013): that paper is about composting.
- Digestate "replacing synthetic N and P fertiliser": the thesis talks about compost for energy crops.
- Microwave pretreatment, Reneron, Green Genius: cited to sources Hermes did not open.
- US (Brown and Li 2013) and Indian (Panigrahi et al. 2020) yard-waste papers: not Lithuanian; excluded.
