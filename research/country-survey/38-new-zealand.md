## Summary

New Zealand's only large AD plant, Ecogas Reporoa, digests food waste: "75,000 tonnes of organic waste, from businesses and kerbside food scrap collections" (operating for Auckland "since October 2021"). The notable yard-waste item is Christchurch: Ecogas won the council contract "to provide an organics processing solution for kerbside and garden organics", with a Hornby facility that "combines anaerobic digestion (AD) technology and biomass processing", consented August 2025 and "on track to start processing organic waste from early 2027". How garden organics are split between the AD and biomass lines: NOT FOUND. Massey University (Ralphs, 2012) tested leaching as AD pretreatment: shredded green waste leachate gave "0.23 ± 0.01" vs grass "0.534 ± 0.005 m3 biogas / kg COD", the green-waste gap possibly due to "tannins".

## Verified findings

- **Ecogas Reporoa — food-waste AD** — Ecogas, "Reporoa Organics Processing Facility" (https://www.ecogas.co.nz/reporoa): "New Zealand's first large-scale food waste-to-bioenergy facility at Reporoa … turns 75,000 tonnes of organic waste, from businesses and kerbside food scrap collections throughout the North Island, into sustainable, renewable energy products. The facility uses anaerobic digestion". Garden waste in feed: NOT FOUND.
- **Ecogas Christchurch (Hornby) — kerbside and garden organics, AD + biomass processing** — Ecogas news, 15 Aug 2025 (https://www.ecogas.co.nz/news/canterbury-resource-consent): "Ecogas was awarded the contract from Christchurch City Council in December 2023 to provide an organics processing solution for kerbside and garden organics. The proposed Hornby facility combines anaerobic digestion (AD) technology and biomass processing"; "on track to start processing organic waste from early 2027"; consent conditions include "Limits on the types of organic material the plant can process"; Reporoa figures "75,000 tonnes … (scaling to 100,000 tonnes by 2026)", "600 GJ of biomethane produced daily"; research partners "Scion and Lincoln University". Capacity of the Christchurch plant and whether garden organics enter the digester: NOT FOUND.
- **Massey University — leaching green waste for AD** — Ralphs J.L., "Improving soluble chemical oxygen demand yields for anaerobic digester feedstock using leaching", MEng thesis, Massey University, 2012 (https://mro.massey.ac.nz/handle/10179/4821): "Pilot trials of shredded green waste and grass leachate reached a maximum total COD yields of 53 ± 2 and 410 ± 20 kg COD / tonne VS respectively"; "Gas production from anaerobic digestion of shredded green waste and grass in a CSTR at 35°C produced 0.23 ± 0.01 and 0.534 ± 0.005 m3 biogas / kg COD respectively"; lower green-waste yield "may be a result of an inhibiting compounds such as tannins". Per kg COD of leachate, not per kg VS of feed.
