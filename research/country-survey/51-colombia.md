## Summary

No Colombian full-scale digester running mainly on yard, grass or pruning waste was found. The evidence is laboratory and planning work. Fresh grass cut from public green areas in Palmira (Valle del Cauca) gave 0.327 m³ CH4/kg VS added in a 60-day BMP test (UNAL Palmira), and the same group estimated that all of Palmira's fruit/vegetable and pruning waste could give about 5,489 MWh of heat or 3,295 MWh of electricity a year. The national UPME-UNAL biogas potential study (Feb 2018) tested grass clippings in the lab but did not rank them among its five priority biomasses; it lists a UAESP dry-digester pilot in Bogotá for market, pruning and grass-cut waste, designed for 705.6 m³ of biogas a month at COP 294,307,978, the most expensive case per MMBTU in its table. In Sabana Centro, co-digesting municipal grass waste with food waste raised biogas by 61 % (Universidad de La Sabana). Bogotá's IDECA/UAESP/UNAL project (2025) built a GIS method to site micro biogas plants for urban tree-pruning waste. By law, grass-cutting and pruning waste from public areas is part of the public cleaning service and should "where possible" go to recovery (Decreto 2981 de 2013, Art. 42).

## Verified findings

- **Fresh grass from public green areas (measured BMP)** — Cadavid Rodríguez L.S. & Bolaños Valencia I.V., "Grass from public green spaces an alternative source of renewable energy in tropical countries" / "Pasto de las áreas verdes públicas como fuente alternativa de energía renovable en países tropicales", Revista ION 29(1) 2016, 109–116, doi 10.18273/revion.v29n1-2016009 (SciELO: http://www.scielo.org.co/scielo.php?pid=S0120-100X2016000100010&script=sci_arttext). Grass from the UNAL Palmira campus, BMP at 37 °C for 60 days: 0.327 m³ CH4/kg VS added ("0,327±2" in the Spanish text), 44 % VS removal, highest yield at 4 % TS. Read via Crossref and a search-index copy; the SciELO server returned errors to direct fetches.
- **Palmira city potential (measured BMP + estimate)** — Cadavid Rodríguez L.S., "Aprovechamiento de residuos orgánicos para la producción de energía renovable en una ciudad colombiana", Energética 46 (2015) (https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/energetica/article/view/46142). Fruit/vegetable waste 0.710 and pruning waste 0.327 m³ CH4/kg VS added; VS removal 48 % and 39 %; 45 % cellulose, 12 % hemicellulose, 4 % lignin removed from pruning waste. All Palmira FV + pruning waste → 5,489 MWh thermal or 3,295 MWh electric (source writes "5.489" and "3.295", Colombian dot thousands).
- **National study (UPME-UNAL, 2018)** — "Estimación del potencial de conversión a biogás de la biomasa en Colombia y su aprovechamiento", Contrato 001 de 2017 UPME-UNAL, Informe Final, Bogotá, February 2018 (https://bdigital.upme.gov.co/bitstream/handle/001/1317/Informe%20final.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1). Grass clippings ("Pasto – Residuos de césped") were one of 10 biomasses characterised and tested at 37 °C, but not one of the 5 prioritised (poultry manure, palm-oil lagoon water, OFMSW, pig manure, vinasse). Table 30: "Residuos Plaza de Mercado, Poda y corte de césped" — UAESP pilot dry digester, Bogotá, from design data: 705.6 m³ biogas/month, investment COP 294,307,978, COP 399,628 per MMBTU (the highest in the table).
- **Grass co-digestion (Sabana Centro)** — Franceschi F.F., Acosta-González A., Vega L.T., Gómez M.F., "Improving dry anaerobic methane production from OFMSW by co-digestion with grass waste and pretreatment with white rot fungi", Energy for Sustainable Development (2023) 372–380, doi 10.1016/j.esd.2023.04.015 (abstract; paywalled), from the PhD thesis of F.F. Franceschi García, Universidad de La Sabana, 2023 (https://hdl.handle.net/10818/55644). Co-digestion with municipal grass waste raised biogas by 61 % for OFMSW without meat (Chía) and was "always … necessary" for OFMSW with meat (Cajicá); white-rot fungal pretreatment did not raise yields.
- **Bogotá pruning-waste siting (method)** — IDECA, "Metodologías basadas en GIS para el diseño de generadores de energía de biomasa urbana" (2025), partners Universidad Nacional de Colombia, IDECA data-analytics office and UAESP (https://ideca.gov.co/uso-de-datos/inspirate/proyectos-de-idi/biomasa). GIS plus multicriteria siting of micro biogas plants for Bogotá's urban tree-pruning waste, to cut disposal at the Doña Juana landfill. Method only; no plant.
- **Manizales pruning waste (desk estimate)** — Vanegas Cárdenas E., Mariscal Moreno J.P., Camargo Valero M.A., Aristizábal Zuluaga B.H., "Digestión anaerobia de residuos de poda como alternativa para disminuir emisiones de gases de efecto invernadero en rellenos sanitarios", Energética 46 (2015) (https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/energetica/article/view/51305). Theoretical comparison: digesting Manizales pruning waste instead of landfilling it would avoid about 50 t CH4 and 1,200 t CO2 (as CO2e) a year.
- **Tree-pruning biochar in digestion (UNAL Medellín)** — Chejne F. et al., "Hybrid polygeneration scheme (pyrolysis, gasification and biological) for producing biochar and biogas from organic waste", Biochar IV conference abstract (https://dc.engconfintl.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1071&context=biochar_iv&filename=0&type=additional): biochar from tree pruning added to digestion of restaurant/community waste gave "20% more biogas … than a conventional system". Related MSc thesis: Ortiz Cardona M., UNAL Medellín 2023 (https://repositorio.unal.edu.co/items/0db57805-3bdd-4f40-978e-b4815070c970/full): palm-kernel-shell biochar raised CH4 per g VS by 44–53 % for cellulose and 12.6 % for restaurant waste; pruning waste was a biochar feedstock, not the digester feed.
- **Law** — Decreto 2981 de 2013 regulates the public cleaning service; Art. 42: pruning and grass-cutting waste from public areas must be collected by the cleaning provider and "En lo posible estos residuos deben destinarse a procesos de aprovechamiento" (https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=56035). Ley 1715 de 2014 gives FNCE incentives (cited in the UPME report). No Colombian rule aimed at yard-waste digestion was found.

## Simulators and modeling tools

Simulators found: none after check. Hermes listed six tools. Four were invented or unsupported: an "IWA ADM1-Matlab" GitHub repo (404), a "GP-CSTR" repo (404, which Hermes itself called hypothetical), an "IDECA biogas-GIS" repo (404), and BioWin and SimaPro (real products, but not mentioned in the Colombian sources Hermes credited). The only real use found is a Colombian Frontiers study (Amado et al. 2021, coffee/cocoa mucilage and swine manure) that built ADM1-based models in the commercial Aspen Plus V9. Aspen Plus is general flowsheet software, not a Colombian digester simulator, so it was not added.

## Not taken (unverified or contradicted)

- "Decreto 2981 de 2013 regulates environmental licensing for biogas plants": wrong (see Law).
- "UPME report (2024) cites national biogas targets and Ley 2099 de 2021": the report is from February 2018 and cites neither.
- A "planned 2 MW Santander biogas plant" with garden waste and "Biogás Colombia S.A.S." as EPC contractor: the cited repository link is dead; the real Santander thesis (Mälardalen University, 2023) studies bagasse, manure and OFMSW.
- "UNAL Manizales bench-scale digester": the Manizales paper is a calculation.
- "Cassava leaves 1.01 m³ CH4/kg" (Sucre): the table is not readable in the open copy, and cassava leaves are a crop residue, not yard waste (Cardona Alzate et al., Waste Biomass Valor. 16, 85–103, online 1 May 2024, UNAL and Universidad de Sucre).
- AnMBR, HTC and membrane upgrading credited to the Frontiers paper: not in it.
- Wrong bibliographic details: "Rodríguez et al.", "Gutiérrez et al. 2020, Universidad de Antioquia", "Gómez-Pineda et al. 2022", "Vásquez et al.", "Universidad del Sinú"; DOI 10.15446/energetica.2015.51305 (does not exist) and two truncated placeholder DOIs.
- UCM review "Serrato et al. 2024": repository blocked automated access; not verified, not used.

NOT FOUND: a Colombian full-scale plant digesting mainly yard, grass or pruning waste; operating data from the UAESP Bogotá dry-digester pilot; a Colombian law or incentive aimed at yard-waste digestion; a Colombian-developed released digester simulator.
