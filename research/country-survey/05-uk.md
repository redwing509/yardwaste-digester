## Summary

The UK has a large wet-AD sector (ADBA, Jan 2024: 723 operational plants, all wet AD), fed mainly by food waste, farm material and crops. Garden waste is composted, not digested: separately collected garden waste "should be sent for composting, typically open windrow" under England's Simpler Recycling guidance, and operators such as Severn Trent Green Power and Veolia digest food waste but compost garden waste. England's first dry-AD plants for mixed food and garden waste were proposed by Greater Manchester (GMCA) in January 2024, but as of January 2026 none had been built or contracted; the business case is being updated after the Green Gas Support Scheme deadline was extended to 2030. The best UK measured data on garden-waste AD come from Leeds/Sheffield lab work: in semi-continuous digesters, green waste gave about 0.175 L CH4/g VS added, against 0.449 for food waste at the same COD loading, and the green-waste run was stopped early by repeated foaming.

## Verified findings

- **Leeds / Sheffield, ADM1 for green and food waste** (Poggio D., Walker M., Nimmo W., Ma L., Pourkashanian M., "Modelling the anaerobic digestion of solid organic waste – Substrate characterisation method for ADM1 using a combined biochemical and kinetic parameter estimation approach", Waste Management 53:40–54, 2016, doi:10.1016/j.wasman.2016.04.024; open copy http://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/102448/). Source-segregated domestic green waste and food waste. Semi-continuous 2 L lab digesters at equal COD loading: green waste 0.123 L CH4/g COD added (0.175 L/g VS added), 0.17 L CH4/L/day; food waste 0.280 L/g COD (0.449 L/g VS), 0.60 L/L/day. The green-waste digester was stopped early because of repeated foaming. For batch data, green waste was best described by one particulate plus one soluble fraction. ADM1 was run in AQUASIM 2.1d.
- **Camley Street Natural Park micro-AD, London** (Walker M. et al., "Assessment of micro-scale anaerobic digestion for management of urban organic waste: A case study in London, UK", Waste Management 61:258–268, 2017, doi:10.1016/j.wasman.2017.01.036; open accepted version https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/112403/). 2 m3 digester (Methanogen UK Ltd) built in 2013; over 319 days in 2014 it took 4,574 kg of **food and catering waste** and made 1,008 m3 biogas at 60.6% CH4, i.e. 595.5 m3 CH4/t VS (0.596 m3/kg VS) and 220 m3 biogas/t fresh matter. A scale reference for small urban digesters; garden waste was not a feedstock.
- **Greater Manchester dry-AD plan** (ADBA press release, 17 Jan 2024, https://adbioresources.org/newsroom/development-of-first-dry-anaerobic-digestion-plants-in-england-on-the-table-as-part-of-greater-manchester-local-waste-management-strategy-a-fantastic-breakthrough-for-our-industry/; GMCA Waste and Recycling Committee minutes, 21 Jan 2026, https://democracy.greatermanchester-ca.gov.uk/documents/s42202/4B%20GM%20Waste%20Recycling%20Committee%20Minutes%2021.1.26.pdf). In Jan 2024 GMCA's committee approved an option for two dry-AD plants, which would be England's first. ADBA estimates 1 Mt of garden waste ≈ 1.0 TWh and puts available garden waste at 6.8 Mt in 2021. GMCA then sought market interest in two 100 kt/yr dry-AD or in-vessel composting facilities; bidders reported few suitable sites and too little time for the GGSS 2028 operation deadline. In Jan 2026, after the GGSS extension, an updated dry-AD business case was approved. No operator was named and no plant had been built.
- **Severn Trent Green Power, Cassington and Wallingford** (https://www.stgreenpower.co.uk/where-we-operate/cassington-ad-facility; GSE case study https://gsecasestudies.org.uk/severn_trent_green_power.php). Combined 97,000 t/yr of food waste, including Oxfordshire household food waste, with PAS 110 biofertiliser and gas to grid or electricity. Cassington's permit allows up to 60,000 t/yr, with three 4,200 m3 primary digesters and 72 °C/1 h pasteurisation. Oxfordshire's **garden waste goes to STGP composting sites, not to AD**.
- **Veolia UK** (https://www.veolia.co.uk/about-us/veolia-uk/recycling-reuse-and-treatment-facilities/composting-facilities): 5 open-windrow sites (>100,000 t/yr green and garden waste) and 2 in-vessel composting sites. No Veolia garden-waste AD plant was found.
- **Cranfield University** (Liu Yanxin, PhD thesis, 2024, https://dspace.lib.cranfield.ac.uk/items/adecb97b-8ee9-48a9-bbc1-bfb521b65567): machine-learning prediction of BMP kinetics and Bayesian ADM1 calibration from early digester data. Methods work, not garden-waste specific.
- **UCL / Surrey LCA** (Evangelisti S., Lettieri P., Borello D., Clift R., Waste Management 34:226–237, 2014, doi:10.1016/j.wasman.2013.09.013; abstract via PubMed 24112851): food-waste AD came out best for CO2 and SO2 savings compared with incineration and landfill (Greater London data). Full text not opened.

## Policy

- **Simpler Recycling, England** (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/simpler-recycling-household-recycling-in-england; Environment Act 2021 s.57, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2021/30/section/57). Since 31 Mar 2026, councils must collect food and garden waste separately from other streams. Garden waste is collected only if the household asks and pays; food waste must be weekly and free. If food and garden waste are co-collected, the service must be weekly and may go to in-vessel composting or "forms of dry anaerobic digestion". Separate garden waste should go to composting, typically open windrow. Some councils have transition dates for food waste.
- **Anaerobic digestate resource framework** (Environment Agency, 20 Oct 2025, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/anaerobic-digestate-resource-framework). End-of-waste for digestate from source-segregated biowaste, with inputs and processing per BSI PAS 110 (2014). Storage is limited to 10 months, with supply contracts or 12-month sales history. Plastic may be at most 8% of the PAS 110 total physical-contaminant limit.
- **Green Gas Support Scheme** (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-gas-support-scheme-ggss; Ofgem). Tariff for biomethane injected to the grid. New applications run to 31 Mar 2028; the commissioning deadline was extended to 31 Mar 2030. This extension is what reopened the GMCA dry-AD case.

## Simulators and modeling tools

Simulators found (after check):

- **AQUASIM**: developer Peter Reichert, Eawag (Switzerland). General simulation and parameter-estimation program for reactors and aquatic systems, with user-defined models such as ADM1. Free since 2013, with executables and source code on the official page; licence name not stated. https://www.eawag.ch/en/department/siam/software/. UK use: Poggio et al. 2016 (Leeds/Sheffield) ran ADM1 for green and food waste in AQUASIM 2.1d. This is a checker find; Hermes did not list it.
- **IWA ADM1 Matlab/Simulink implementations** (wwtmodels/Anaerobic-Digestion-Models): developers Lund University and DTU (Flores-Alsina, Rosen, Jeppsson, Gernaey and others). ADM1 variants for Matlab/Simulink (BSM2 default, DAE pH/H2, ionic corrections, P-S-Fe). No licence file in the repository, and Matlab/Simulink is required. https://github.com/wwtmodels/Anaerobic-Digestion-Models. Model paper: Rosen et al., Water Sci. Technol. 54(4):11–19 (2006). Hermes listed it as a UK tool, but **UK use was not confirmed**; it is not UK-developed.

Not taken: the "Leeds/Sheffield ADM1 substrate characterisation tool" (a published method, not software); "Aspen Plus + ADM1" (Southampton 2014 thesis is real, but its author could not be confirmed and Aspen Plus is a generic commercial tool); a "Cranfield BMP predictor (Python notebooks)" (thesis real, no code release found); HOMER (commercial microgrid tool, claimed UK AD use not found); a "Veolia SEPURAN membrane model" (cited page 404). Nothing was installed.
