## Summary

Japan has a long record of anaerobic digestion research on woody and green residues, but almost all of it is lab, pilot or demonstration scale, and yard waste is nearly always a minor co-feedstock. Documented work: Kyoto City's early-2000s demonstration plant (hotel food waste, yard waste, used paper); a forestry institute (FFPRI) pilot that digests wet-milled wood directly (needs about 10% animal protein for stable operation); the Public Works Research Institute (PWRI) work on grass clippings and steam-exploded oak chips co-digested with sewage sludge; and low pruning-branch yields reported by Fuji Tokoha University. A government (NEDO) guide states that pruning branches are poor biogas feedstock and unsuited to methane fermentation. Municipal biogas (e.g. Maniwa City) runs mainly on food waste and night soil, not yard waste.

## Verified findings

- **Kyoto City demonstration plant** (Komatsu T. et al., Water Science and Technology 45(12):113–118, June 2002, doi:10.2166/wst.2002.0416; authors from Kubota, IHI, Kawasaki Heavy Industries, Takuma). Abstract (Crossref): operated about two years; hotel garbage and leftovers, yard waste and used paper mixed at various ratios; stable operation with each mixture; biogas about 820 Nm3/t-VS decomposed. The "57.7% methane" in the draft is not in the abstract and was not taken.
- **FFPRI wood methane fermentation pilot, Odaka, Minamisoma, Fukushima** (Fukushima Prefecture project, Oct 2016–Sep 2017). Wet-milling turns wood into slurry; thermophilic digester at 55 C; stable long-run operation required adding animal protein at about 1/10 of the wood. Cedar bark about 400 L biogas/kg and red pine about 500 L biogas/kg; methane about 60%; radioactive caesium did not transfer to the gas. Sources: MAFF Agri-Researcher record (https://agresearcher.maff.go.jp/seika/show/238618), FFPRI summary (https://www.ffpri.go.jp/research/saizensen/2020/20200131-03.html), paper Navarro et al., Bioresource Technology 300:122622 (2020), doi:10.1016/j.biortech.2019.122622. Pilot scale is 500 L per FFPRI.
- **Public Works Research Institute (PWRI), grass clippings and wood chips with sewage sludge.** PWRI project report 6.3 (FY2014; https://www.pwri.go.jp/jpn/results/report/report-project/2014/pdf/pro-6-3.pdf): grass clippings (cut to about 10 mm) co-digested with sewage sludge gave more methane than separate digestion; steam-exploded Quercus serrata (konara) chips reached about 50% methane conversion even at the milder 0.9 MPa / 178 C (conventional: 2.0 MPa / 213 C); thermophilic slightly better than mesophilic; an estimate that collecting clippings within about 31 km of a plant with an existing digester is climate-beneficial. Journal paper: Wang F., Hidaka T., Sakurai K., Tsumori J., Bioresource Technology 166 (Aug 2014), "Anaerobic co-digestion of steam-treated Quercus serrata chips and sewage sludge under mesophilic and thermophilic conditions" (https://thesis.pwri.go.jp/public_detail/120028): apparent methane yield 248–271 dm3/kg VS for steam-treated chips with sludge; untreated chips inhibited methane production; methane conversion 40.5–53.8% (mesophilic) and 49.0–63.7% (thermophilic) in batch tests.
- **Fuji Tokoha University / Univ. of Shizuoka**: Yamamoto K., Murakami A., Iwahori K., "Biogas and Methane Yields from Paper Sludge by Anaerobic Digestion", Japanese Journal of Water Treatment Biology 44(2):77–86 (2008), doi:10.2521/jswtb.44.77. The paper's subject is paper sludge. Pruning branches were also digested and gave very low methane (about 0.02–0.09 m3/kg-VS mesophilic, 0.05–0.07 thermophilic), and were left out of the kinetic analysis. The pruning figures come from a search summary of the paper; the J-STAGE page itself returned an error when opened, so treat the pruning numbers as likely but not directly re-read.
- **Konoike batch dry AD (Konoike Construction)**: Takemori H., Shioji I., Nakajima T., "バッチ式乾式メタン発酵における発酵促進効果の検証", 36th Annual Meeting, Japan Society of Material Cycles and Waste Management (17–19 Sep 2025), J-STAGE abstract: the company is introducing Japan's first batch-type dry methane fermentation plant; small-scale tests showed batch dry gives about 89% of the biogas of wet digestion; biochar (DIET) tested on dairy manure and food residue. The draft's "pruning branches + cattle manure, Shioji & Nakajima 2008, J. Solid Waste Technol. Manag. 35:309-321" was not found and was replaced by this source.
- **Nara Prefecture Livestock Technology Center batch dry AD trial (FY2005)**, MAFF Agri-Researcher (https://agresearcher.maff.go.jp/kadai/show/122021): pig manure + pruning branches seed; test batch of cattle manure 1.26 t, poultry manure 2.02 t, pruning branches 1.75 t plus seed and sludge at 65% moisture, 40 C; methane in the fermenter headspace reached 22.9% by day 13; H2S 2,200 ppm.
- **NEDO guide (Part 3, methane fermentation basics; https://www.nedo.go.jp/content/100932093.pdf)**: pruning branches "cannot be expected to produce much gas and are not suited to methane fermentation" (depends on leaf content); dry fermentation TS in Japan is about 10% and above; Japanese dry-AD record as of March 2019: Kurita DRANCO 1 plant, KOMPOGAS 4 plants; batch-type dry (BEKON-style) 0 plants in Japan then.
- **Maniwa City (Okayama)**: the draft's "5 t/day food waste with night soil" is a mix-up of two facilities. The 2015–2017 demonstration plant took about 4.9 t/day (food waste, cattle manure, fishery waste, night soil and septic sludge), 210 m3 mesophilic wet digester, 25 kW CHP; Dec 2016: biogas 48,959 Nm3/yr at about 60% CH4, 39,566 kWh/yr electricity, 1,351 t/yr digestate sprayed on paddies and vegetables (Ministry of the Environment page, https://www.env.go.jp/content/900498010.pdf). The current "Manikurun" Resource Circulation Center takes food waste 10 t/day, night soil 19 kL/day and septic sludge 76 kL/day, wet mesophilic, 6 x 25 kW gas engines, and concentrates the digestate into liquid fertilizer (https://maniwa-nogyo.com/meguri/recyclingcenter/). No yard waste feedstock is stated.
- **Wood-waste NaOH + pig manure study** is not Japanese and was mis-cited. The 75.8% figure is from Catalysts 9(6):539 (2019), "Evaluation on the Methane Production Potential of Wood Waste Pretreated with NaOH and Co-Digested with Pig Manure" (https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4344/9/6/539): NaOH pretreatment alone +38.5%; NaOH-pretreated wood with pig manure +75.8% versus untreated wood (309.06 mL CH4/g VS). The draft's "Chemical Engineering Journal 487:150496" is actually a different paper (a machine-learning meta-analysis of wood waste in AD, doi:10.1016/j.cej.2024.150496, open access CC BY).
