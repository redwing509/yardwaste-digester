## Summary

Italy has a large and growing biowaste-AD sector, but green waste mostly goes to composting. ISPRA (2022 data) counts 358 biological treatment plants: 285 composting-only, 51 integrated anaerobic/aerobic and 22 AD-only. Green waste is about a third of composting input (>1.2 Mt), but only 403 kt (11%) of the ~3.7 Mt going to integrated AD/aerobic plants, where food waste dominates. Even in new integrated plants, green waste is typically used as **bulking agent in the composting stage, not as digester feed**: Rome's AMA Cesano plant (VIA opinion, Dec 2023) digests 100,000 t/yr of food and market waste in thermophilic wet digesters and adds 10,000 t/yr of public-green cuttings only to the digestate composting biocells. No Italian full-scale plant digesting mainly green waste was found. The only green-waste-only Italian lab work found (Urbino/Florence, 2021–2022) characterised biogas composition and VOCs; no methane yield figure was published in the sources opened.

## Verified findings

- **AMA STZ Cesano, Rome** (VIA technical opinion, Prot. RM/2023/0003861, 7 Dec 2023, https://commissari.gov.it/media/tg0pbwyc/rm20230003861-all-1_parere-tecnico-istruttorio_via_ce_def.pdf). Proponent AMA SpA (Rome's municipal waste company). 110,000 t/yr: 100,000 t/yr kitchen, canteen and market organics plus 10,000 t/yr public-green maintenance waste (EER 20 02 01) and wood. AD: 4 × 4,700 m3 digesters at 52–55 °C, 35 days; 15.7 million Nm3/yr biogas at 55–67% CH4, upgraded and liquefied for vehicle fuel. Solid digestate (17,584 t/yr) is mixed with the 10,000 t/yr of green cuttings and branches as structuring material and composted in 10 aerated biocells, giving 14,948 t/yr mature compost. **Green waste is composted, not digested.** A twin plant is planned at Casal Selce (200,000 t/yr for both). An AMA 2025 report snippet gives ~5,353 t/yr liquid biomethane (not opened in full).
- **ISPRA, Rapporto Rifiuti Urbani 2023** (data year 2022, https://www.isprambiente.gov.it/files2023/pubblicazioni/rapporti/rapportorifiutiurbani_ed-2023_n-393_versioneintegrale.pdf). 358 plants (285 composting, 51 integrated AD/aerobic, 22 AD). Integrated plants: ~3.7 Mt treated (food waste ~3 Mt, 81.4%; green waste 403 kt, 11.0%); >331 million Nm3 biogas and >153 million Nm3 biomethane (91.9% to transport); 23 plants upgrade to biomethane (13 in 2021). Green waste going to composting fell by ~134 kt (−9.8%) in 2021–2022 as some composting plants converted to integrated AD/aerobic. AD-only plants produced 333 kt digestate.
- **Urbino / Florence green-waste lab digestion** (Folino, Randazzo, Tassi, Tatàno, De Rosa, Gambioli, Proc. SARDINIA 2021, record https://hdl.handle.net/11576/2692911; and Randazzo et al., Detritus 19:63–74, 2022, doi:10.31025/2611-4135/2022.15188, open PDF). Mesophilic (38 °C) 49-day batch BMP of park/garden green waste (50.1% TS, VS 67.1% of TS, substrate:inoculum 2:1 on VS) with the ASET Fano utility. Biogas carried up to 29 VOCs, mainly terpenes, relevant for gas engines and landfill gas. The authors note Italian green waste goes mainly to composting. **No methane yield figure in the opened texts.**
- **Florence / AISA Impianti, wet vs dry-batch AD** (Francini, Lasagni, Lombardi, Detritus 9:94–104, 2020, doi:10.31025/2611-4135/2020.13921, CC BY-NC-ND). Desk techno-economic comparison for a 35,000 Mg/yr source-sorted organic-waste plant with upgrading and composting. Dry batch had a ~21% lower primary-energy saving but better CO2-eq, higher NPV and shorter payback; Monte Carlo gave a 90% chance of better economics than wet AD. Relevant to dry-batch designs that can take more woody material.
- **ENEA micro-digester, Sicily** (Baldi, Sdringola, Beozzo, Di Pietra, Renewable Energy 251:123333, 2025, doi:10.1016/j.renene.2025.123333; open copy on ENEA IRIS). Two 2,000 L HomeBiogas units at a farmhouse in Misiliscemi (Trapani), May 2023–Feb 2024, fed **kitchen/food waste**: 601 kg wet feed → 145 m3 biogas at 53% CH4, 84 kg CH4/t organic matter, ~170 L/d per digester; output rose ~1.2 L/h per °C. A small-digester scale reference, not garden waste.
- **ENEA hydrogenotrophic methanation** (ENEA EAI "Pianeta Idrogeno" article, https://www.eai.enea.it/archivio/pianeta-idrogeno/the-role-of-hydrogen-in-the-circular-management-of-wastewater-treatment-plants.html). Biological upgrading with H2 and hydrodynamic cavitation (+GAS project): 0.20 m3 CH4/m3 H2 and >98% CH4 in the reactor gas. Lab/pilot, wastewater context.
- **Politecnico di Milano ADM1 calibration** (Catenacci, Grana, Malpei, Ficara, Water 13:3100, 2021, doi:10.3390/w13213100, open copy on Polimi RE). Calibration protocol for a 60 L pilot co-digesting waste sludge and expired yogurt; ADM1 run in OpenModelica. Methods only; not green waste.

## Policy

- **Biomethane decree, DM 15 Sep 2022** (MASE; GSE pages). PNRR M2C2 investment 1.4: 40% capital grant plus a 15-year tariff on net biomethane, allocated by competitive auctions; covers agricultural and "organic-waste" plants (FORSU plus matrices listed in D.Lgs. 199/2021 Annex VIII Part A). Requires ≥65% GHG savings for transport use, ≥80% for other uses. Whether pure green waste qualifies on its own was not confirmed.
- **PNRR M2C1 investment 1.1, Line B** (MASE; €450 M national call): funds composting of FORSU mixed with green waste and AD plants (dry, wet or mixed) with biogas capture.
- Background laws named in the draft, not opened: D.Lgs. 152/2006 (environmental code), D.Lgs. 75/2010 (fertilisers).
