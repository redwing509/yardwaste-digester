## Summary

No Saudi digester running on yard, park or street green waste was found, and no Saudi lab test on urban grass or prunings. The nearest measured work is a 2014 lab study that digested crushed whole dead date-palm trees from Hofuf: after 0.75 % NaOH pretreatment it gave 342.2 L biogas per kg VS fed (up to 50 % methane) in a 5 L digester at 40 °C. KAUST's own food and green waste goes to composting (Edama, about 4,500 m³ of soil improver), not digestion. National studies are desk estimates: anaerobic digestion of food waste could give up to 2.99 TWh a year (Energies 2026), and manure, farm and urban waste together about 1.4 billion m³ of biogas and 3,000 GWh of electricity a year (Arabian Journal for Science and Engineering 2025, Turkish authors). Saudi Arabia targets 3 GW of waste-to-energy capacity by 2030. The 2021 Waste Management Law ranks recycling, then resource recovery, then safe disposal, but says nothing specific about green waste or biogas. Farm date-palm fronds are a large separate stream (over 800,000 t a year from about 32 million palms, mostly burned).

## Verified findings

- **Date-palm tree waste (lab AD)** — Al-Juhaimi F.Y., Hamad S.H., Al-Ahaideb I.S., Al-Otaibi M.M., Ghafoor K., Abbasi T., Abbasi S.A., "Biogas Production through the Anaerobic Digestion of Date Palm Tree Wastes – Process Optimization", BioResources 9(2) 2014, 3323–3333, doi 10.15376/biores.9.2.3323-3333 (https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/resources/biogas-production-through-the-anaerobic-digestion-of-date-palm-tree-wastes-process-optimization/). King Saud University, King Faisal University (Al-Ahsa), Imam Muhammad bin Saud University, Pondicherry University. Feedstock: whole dead palms crushed into strips (Hofuf disposal site; not stated as urban or farm). Best: alkali pretreatment, 2–5 mm, C:N 30:1, 40 °C, pH 7, ~10 % VS, 5 L digester. 342.2 L biogas/kg VS fed (0.75 % NaOH; ~326 and ~320 at 1.0 and 1.25 %), max/avg 674.5 / 404.4 L/m³/day, up to 50 % CH4, 58 % organic-matter reduction; cellulose −68.7 %, hemicellulose −73.4 %; residue a potential soil fertilizer.
- **National AD potential (food waste)** — Abushammala M.F.M., Almuaythir S., Al-Zghoul T.M., Alazaiza M.Y.D., "Waste-to-Energy Technologies in Saudi Arabia: A Case Study and Review of Waste Conversion and Energy Recovery", Energies 19(4) 1085, 2026, doi 10.3390/en19041085 (https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/4/1085). About 16 Mt MSW a year, 37–57 % organic; AD biogas 60–80 % methane, "potentially yielding up to 2.99 TWh annually" — from optimized AD of food waste; waste rises in Hajj and Ramadan.
- **National biomass potential (estimate)** — Alshekh Zein O. & Aksay M.V. (Karabuk University, Turkey), "The Present Energy Status in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia and the Potential of Biomass Energy in Reducing Greenhouse Gas Emissions", Arab J Sci Eng 51(14) 17319–17333, online 15 Dec 2025, doi 10.1007/s13369-025-10976-3 (abstract). Over 5 Mt manure, 0.46 Mt agricultural waste, 16.5 Mt urban solid waste a year; ~1.4 billion m³ biogas and 3,000 GWh electricity.
- **Biomass AD potential + straw co-digestion (Shaqra)** — Alghubaywi T.H., Abdel-Daiem M.M. (Shaqra University), Said N. (Zagazig University), "Enhancing Energy Recovery from Anaerobic Digestion of Biomass Wastes in Saudi Arabia", IEEE conference, Shaqra, Dec 2024, doi 10.1109/ieeeconf63577.2024.10881014 (abstract). About 8,355.21 GWh/yr electricity (household, sewage, animal, crop residues; theoretical). Waste activated sludge co-digested with NaOH-pretreated straw: 6-fold biogas vs untreated straw.
- **KAUST green waste → compost, not AD** — KAUST news, "Edama opens new waste recycling facility", 7 Aug 2022 (https://www.kaust.edu.sa/en/news/edama-opens-new-waste-recycling-facility): will recycle 100 % of KAUST's food and green waste, producing about 4,500 m³ of soil improver; SIRC awarded Edama a larger facility.
- **Financial model (KFUPM)** — Hadidi L.A. & Omer M.M., "A financial feasibility model of gasification and anaerobic digestion waste-to-energy (WTE) plants in Saudi Arabia", Waste Management 59 (2017) 90–101, doi 10.1016/j.wasman.2016.09.030 (abstract; paywalled). NPV, IRR, LCOE, LCOW; for AD the key drivers are facility waste capacity and capacity factor.
- **Policy** — Waste Management Law, Royal Decree No. M/3, 13 Aug 2021 (official translation: https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/07/Waste-Management-Law.pdf): National Center for Waste Management licenses and permits; Art. 5 priority recycling → resource recovery → safe disposal; Art. 37 replaces the MSW law of 17/9/1434H. No green-waste or biogas article. WTE target 3 GW by 2030 (Alghafis et al., Sustainability 17(20) 8980, 2025, doi 10.3390/su17208980).
- **Farm palm fronds (context)** — Alfayez A. (Saudi Investment Recycling Company), ByPalma 2023 abstract (https://faculty.ksu.edu.sa/sites/default/files/ByPalma2023_Book_of_Abstracts_01.12.2023.pdf): date palm waste over 800 thousand t a year from ~32 million palms; most farm owners burn the fronds. Farm residue, studied for particleboard, not digestion.
- Non-yard context: Qassim University kitchen waste + cattle dung in nine 5 L batch digesters (75 % vs 25 % kitchen waste: +17.3 % biogas; Aljumah & Alyahya, IJRASET 2023, doi 10.22214/ijraset.2023.49959); KAUST AnMBR wastewater pilot at MODON Jeddah, 50,000 L/day (KAUST, Oct 2022).
