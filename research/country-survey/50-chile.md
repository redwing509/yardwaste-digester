## Summary

No Chilean digester taking mainly yard, garden or park waste was found. Chilean biogas plants run on manure, industrial wastewater, landfill gas and, in one case, winery residues: VSPT Wine Group's plant at Molina (Maule), built in 2017 with Bio Energía Molina, digests grape pomace, stalks and winery wastewater sludge. Measured winery-residue yields (Montalvo et al. 2020) run from 93–150 L CH4/kg VS for vine shoots up to 876 L CH4/kg VS for wine lees. For yard waste, Chile has only desk estimates. The 2007 CNE/GTZ biogas potential study estimates that municipal pruning and weeding waste in five central regions could give about 425,488 m³ of biogas a year (297,842 m³ CH4), feasible only in large municipalities. A 2017 University of Chile thesis found thermophilic dry digestion of the comuna de Santiago's pruning and garden waste slightly unprofitable (NPV −0.39 million CLP) against in-vessel composting (+42.4 million CLP). Garden residues are only 2 ± 1 % (dry basis) of Bío Bío municipal waste.

## Verified findings

- **Yard-waste biogas potential (national estimate)** — CNE/GTZ, "Potencial de biogás: identificación y clasificación de los distintos tipos de biomasa disponibles en Chile para la generación de biogás", R. Chamy & E. Vivanco (PUCV), Santiago, September 2007, ISBN 978-956-7700-08-0 (https://energypedia.info/images/d/dc/Potencial_Biogas_Chile.pdf). Urban pruning and weeding waste from parks and gardens, extrapolated from Viña del Mar to regions V, RM, VI, VII and VIII: 27,000 m³/yr, about 1,595 t/yr wet. Table 33: 1,593,589 kg/yr → 425,488 m³/yr biogas, 297,842 m³/yr methane (270 m³ biogas per tonne). "Only feasible in municipalities with large areas of parks and gardens." This is a desk estimate, not a measurement.
- **Santiago pruning and garden waste (feasibility thesis)** — A. N. Cueto Codorniú, "Evaluación de tecnologías para la reutilización, valorización y disposición de residuos orgánicos", chemical engineering thesis, Universidad de Chile, 2017 (https://repositorio.uchile.cl/handle/2250/145901, abstract). Base case 630,000 kg/yr of pruning and garden waste. 15-year NPV: in-vessel composting 42.4, thermophilic dry digestion −0.390, gasification −72.8, landfill −73.4 (million CLP). Recommends composting.
- **Winery residues (measured BMP)** — Montalvo S., Martínez J., Castillo A., Huiliñir C., Borja R., García V., Salazar R., Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments 37 (2020) 100640, doi 10.1016/j.seta.2020.100640 (abstract; paywalled; a green-OA postprint is listed at digital.csic.es but could not be read). Batch digestion of stalks, pomace, vine shoots, wastewater sludge and wine lees: wine lees 876 ± 45 and sludge 690 ± 25 L CH4/kg VS (day 57); vine shoots lowest at 93 ± 5 (day 30) and 150 ± 6 (day 57) L CH4/kg VS. Biogas could cover the plant's energy demand.
- **VSPT Molina plant** — VSPT Wine Group's biogas plant at Molina, built in 2017 with Bio Energía Molina, on "orujos y escobajos, y lodos de la planta de Riles", producing heat, power and digestate for its vineyards (Guía Chile Energía, Cero Basura 2023 award). SEC biogas registry: Bio Energía Molina SpA, Molina, Maule, "Grandes > 900 kW", registered 11.09.2017 (https://www.sec.cl/sitio-web/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Plantas-de-Biogas-v1.pdf). No audited throughput or output figure found.
- **Concha y Toro** — No biogas plant found. Its 2018 statement says 62 % of its pre-consumer residues go to tartaric acid and 38 % to compost, out of 43,000 t/yr of pomace and stalks (Emol, 23 May 2018). There is no SEC registry entry.
- **Municipal waste studies** — Coyhaique (Aysén): an LCA of landfill-gas flaring, landfill-gas energy and anaerobic digestion of the organic fraction found landfill-gas energy recovery the "most environmentally appropriate solution" (Bezama et al., Waste Manag. Res. 31(10 Suppl) 2013, 67–74, doi 10.1177/0734242X13499810). Bío Bío: an LCA of landfill flaring, landfill-gas power, incineration and gasification, with no digestion scenario (González, Riveros, Concha, Casas, U. de Concepción, Int. J. Energy Prod. & Mgmt. 3(2) 2018, 144–156, doi 10.2495/EQ-V3-N2-144-156). Its MSW composition (dry basis) is organic matter 54 ± 5 % and garden residues 2 ± 1 %.
- **Law** — Biogas plant safety: Decreto 119/2016, Ministerio de Energía, "Reglamento de Seguridad de las Plantas de Biogás" (published 2 Feb 2017; https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=1099847). SEC Res. Ex. 22.337/2018 sets the biogas plant registration procedure. Ley 20.999 (2017) amends the gas-services law and is not a biogas law. No Chilean law or incentive aimed at yard-waste digestion was found.
