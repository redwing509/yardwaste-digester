## Summary

Ireland already treats more biowaste by anaerobic digestion than by composting: in 2024, of 548,000 t sent to composting and AD, "58% was treated by anaerobic digestion. This is equivalent to 316,000 tonnes" (EPA). Garden and park waste (EWC 20 02) accepted at these plants was 56,258 t in 2024 (EPA data archive); how much of that went to AD rather than composting: NOT FOUND. Ireland's main lignocellulosic AD research is on agricultural grass silage, not yard waste: UCC (Nizami, Murphy et al., 2012) found BMP of one silage "varied from 350 to 493L CH4 kg-1 VS added" by method, and a pilot two-stage CSTR "achieved 451L CH4 kg-1 VS added over a 50day retention period".

## Verified findings

- **EPA Ireland — 2024 composting and AD** — EPA, "Composting & Anaerobic Digestion Statistics for Ireland" (https://www.epa.ie/our-services/monitoring--assessment/waste/national-waste-statistics/composting--anaerobic/): "548,000 tonnes of waste collected in Ireland were treated at composting and anaerobic digestion facilities. 58% was treated by anaerobic digestion. This is equivalent to 316,000 tonnes. 42% underwent composting. This is equivalent to 232,000 tonnes. 335,000 tonnes were municipal waste"; "184,000 tonnes were exported for treatment".
- **EPA data archive — garden and park waste series** — EPA, Compost-and-AD-Data-Archive-Summary-CSV (https://www.epa.ie/our-services/monitoring--assessment/waste/national-waste-statistics/waste-statistics-data-archive/compost-and-anaerobic-digestion-data-archive/Compost-and-AD-Data-Archive-Summary-CSV.csv): row "20 02 - Garden and park wastes (including cemetery waste)", Tonnes, 2018–2024: "46290,45988,54167,60164,54331,52232,56258". Split between compost and AD for this row: NOT FOUND.
- **UCC — grass silage BMP and pilot digesters** — Nizami A.S., Orozco A., Groom E., Dieterich B., Murphy J.D., "How much gas can we get from grass?", *Applied Energy* 92, 783–790, Apr 2012, DOI 10.1016/j.apenergy.2011.08.033 (resolves, 302) (https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/how-much-gas-can-we-get-from-grass/): "methane potential from the same grass silage varied from 350 to 493L CH 4 kg -1 VS added for three different BMP procedures"; "The CSTR system achieved 451L CH 4 kg -1 VS added over a 50day retention period. The SLBR-UASB achieved 341L CH 4 kg -1 VS added at a 30day retention time". Feed is agricultural grass silage, not garden waste.
