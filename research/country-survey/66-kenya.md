## Summary

Kenya has no documented anaerobic digester for municipal yard, park or garden waste. The Sustainable Waste Management Act 2022 makes all public and private entities separate organic from non-organic waste, and names composting (not digestion) as the treatment step at county materials-recovery facilities. Kenyan waste-to-biogas projects take market, food, kitchen, manure, sewage or flower-farm waste: the Ahero and New Ngong market digesters (Flexi Biogas), the planned Umoja estate plant in Nairobi (food waste and cow dung) and the Kisumu market/settlement project (EIB/GIZ study). The nearest case to "green waste to energy" is agricultural: Gorge Farm in Naivasha digests field and packhouse green waste and is described by NEMA as "Africa's first grid connect Anaerobic Digester plant". Garden/green waste that is diverted is composted (TakaTaka Solutions).

## Verified findings

- **Segregation duty, composting route** — Sustainable Waste Management Act, 2022 (Act No. 31 of 2022): ""organic waste" means compostable materials derived from plants and animals;"; s.12(1) "All public and private sector entities shall segregate non-hazardous waste into organic and non-organic fractions."; s.14(2) a materials recovery facility "shall be used for final sorting, segregation, composting and recycling of waste". Anaerobic digestion is not named in these sections. https://nema.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/SWMA-2022.pdf
- **Gorge Farm Energy Park, Naivasha (farm green waste, not municipal yard waste)** — NEMA, *Best Practices In Waste Management In Kenya*: "all the green waste generated from the field and vegetable packing houses from the surroundings is used to generate 5 megawatts of electricity through the Biojoule plant on location"; "This is Africa's first grid connect Anaerobic Digester plant"; electricity sold to Kenya Power "For $0.10/kWh" (figures as printed in the booklet). https://nema.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Environmental-Best-Practices-in-Waste-Management.pdf
- **Flower-farm digesters** — Ministry of Energy and Petroleum (page dated 24 Jul 2023): "400 M3 digester constructed at P. J. Dave Flower Farms Ltd., Isinya to generate 100kW"; "200 M3 digester constructed at Ereka Holdings Ltd, Simbi Roses, Thika to generate55kW"; preliminary results "One (1) ton of flower waste produced 60-80 M3 of biogas" and "One (1) M3 produced 0.94 kWh of power"; JKUAT institutional digester "385 M3". https://energy.go.ke/biogas/
- **Kisumu organic waste (desk estimate)** — Omutto (African Wildlife Foundation), *African Journal of Tropical Hydrobiology and Fisheries* 16: 103–107 (2018): Kisumu "generates about 400 t/day of solid waste of which 60% is organic"; "The organic fraction consists of food and garden wastes"; at an assumed 110 m³ biogas per tonne, 26,400 m³/day and 52,800 kWh/day (arithmetic checked). Table 1 (NEMA 2015): Nairobi 2,400 t/day, Mombasa 2,200, Thika 140, Eldoret 600, Nakuru 250, Kisumu 400. https://www.ajol.info/index.php/ajthf/article/download/174582/163973
- **Measured Kenyan methane potentials (no yard waste)** — Mulat, Ngwa, Chernet & Mwangi (ILRI, 2025): market, household, fish, slaughterhouse waste, water hyacinth and cattle manure gave "230 to 441 mL/g volatile solids (VS)" (summary) but "approximately 326 and 541 mL CH₄/g VS" (conclusion) — the report does not reconcile the two ranges. Yard waste appears only as a guess about market-waste content ("fruits, vegetables, some paper or yard waste"). https://cgspace.cgiar.org/bitstreams/ca060787-1a7b-426b-b48b-b45feb430925/download
- **Market and food-waste plants** — Flexi Biogas: Ahero Market, Kisumu, "over 1 ton of organic waste daily"; New Ngong Market "over 2 tons of waste daily"; Jet Inn Gardens Hotel, Emali, 100 m³ T-REX on kitchen and food waste, "40–50m3 of biogas daily"; Dol Dol, Laikipia: invasive opuntia cactus feeding "over 100 domestic biogas systems". https://www.biogas.co.ke/projects.html . The company brochure claims its systems run on "grass, water hyacinth, farm weed and garden clippings" (vendor claim, no plant record). http://biogas.co.ke/en/Biogas%20International%20Ltd_Brochure_2015.pdf
- **Planned municipal plants** — C40, Umoja estate, Nairobi (May 2019): "fed with food waste from surrounding estates, and it will also use animal waste, such as cow dung"; Nairobi "2,400 tons of waste daily. Out of this, more than 50% is organic." https://www.c40.org/case-studies/umoja-estate-nairobi-turns-organic-waste-into-clean-energy-biogas/ . EIB: the Kisumu project, "still being studied", would take organic waste "from four farmer's markets and six informal settlements" — "16 000 tonnes of waste ... over a four-year period"; technical assistance "about €170,000". https://www.eib.org/en/stories/kenya-biogas-waste-recycling
- **Early pilots** — Carbon Africa (Dec 2010): ARTI compact units on urban organic waste in Nairobi (pilot March–November 2010); in the cold season "colder weather seriously curtails biogas production" (2 hours of gas per day versus 3–4 for the institutional unit). https://edepot.wur.nl/160128 . Eawag/Sandec (C. Müller, May 2007): "Currently there is no working biogas plant treating organic solid waste in Kenya"; a planned 14 m³ digester at Mtwapa for organic waste and "other leafy material from his farm" (JuaNguvu Ltd). https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/Anaerobic_Digestion/Mueller_2007.pdf
- **Green waste is composted** — ENF directory: TakaTaka Solutions accepts "Garden/Green Waste" → "Composts"; Regen Organics 30,000 t/yr and Griincom 480 t/yr organic waste → compost (directory data). https://enforganic.com/directory/plant/Kenya
- **Agricultural residues (Moi University)** — Nzila, J. Energy Technol. Policy 7(2), 2017: CH4 "40% (sugar cane leaves) to 62% (cotton residue)", H2S 105–1100 ppmv; grass named as a good substrate only from foreign literature (Ranalli 2007). https://www.iiste.org/Journals/index.php/JETP/article/download/35537/36557 . Nzila et al., "Biowaste energy potential in Kenya", Renewable Energy 35(12) 2698–2704, 2010 (metadata only; paywalled). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2010.04.016
- **Foreign thesis, Nairobi model** — Kuetche Chombong, MSc thesis, Politecnico di Torino (July 2026): a 1,000-person Nairobi neighbourhood pilot on household, restaurant and market organics (412.8 kg/day, about 24.8 m³ digester, 30-day HRT); no garden-waste share for Nairobi (the thesis's "garden waste making up 27%" is Italy's national figure). https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/41035/1/tesi.pdf
