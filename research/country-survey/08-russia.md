## Summary

No Russian plant that digests garden, park or yard waste was found, and no Russian measured methane yield for grass, leaves or branches was found in open sources (NOT FOUND). Russia has a long sludge-digestion history (Soviet industrial biogas since the 1960s; decades of thermophilic sludge digestion in Moscow), and published national potentials count agricultural plant residues (36.8 billion m3 biogas/yr) and municipal biowaste (2.1 billion m3/yr), but not garden waste on its own. The closest documented Russian plant is a small farm plant in Udmurtia listing grass among its feedstocks. The only lab result with plant residue is a Kazan study where dried amaranth stems added to cow manure raised biogas output by about a third. The 2023 national standard on organic municipal waste (voluntary) covers plant waste from municipal waste and describes AD briefly, but is mainly about composting.

## Verified findings

- **National potential — Kalyuzhnyi 2008** (S. Kalyuzhnyi, "Energy potential of anaerobic digestion of wastes produced in Russia via biogas and microbial fuel cell technologies", Pure Appl. Chem. 80(10):2115–2124, 2008, https://doi.org/10.1351/pac200880102115; full text read as a search snapshot). All agro-industrial wastes: 773 Mt/yr (228 Mt DM) → 62.5 billion m3 biogas/yr and 121 Mt organo-mineral fertiliser. Plant-breeding wastes alone: 36.8 billion m3/yr at 0.25 m3/kg DM. Municipal solid waste: 35 Mt/yr; ~40% easily biodegradable "biowastes" (14 Mt/yr) → 2.1 billion m3 biogas/yr (70% CH4, 0.3 m3/kg DM), provided "a separate collection of biowastes should be organized". The paper's own conversion: 1,000 m3 biogas (70% CH4) = 25 GJ = 4.41 MWh ≈ 500 L gasoline/diesel. No garden-waste category. (Hermes dated it 2022 and said the 62.5 bn m3 covered "all waste streams including garden waste" — wrong.)
- **Amaranth stems + cow manure — Karaeva & Timofeeva 2019** (Yu.V. Karaeva, S.S. Timofeeva, Institute of Power Engineering and Advanced Technologies, Kazan Scientific Centre RAS; Vestnik Ulyanovsk State Agricultural Academy 2019, https://doi.org/10.18286/1816-4501-2019-4-6-10; open text https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/issledovanie-protsessa-anaerobnogo-sbrazhivaniya-koroviego-navoza-i-rastitelnyh-othodov). Lab reactor, 37 °C; dried, ground amaranth stems and sawdust added to cow manure as carriers to immobilise microbes, with ultrasonic treatment. Peak biogas (not methane) 7.82 mL/g VS with *Amaranthus cruentus* stems vs 5.92 mL/g VS in the control (+32.3%); *A. retroflexus* 6.32 mL/g VS (+6.8%); sawdust cut biogas by 39.4%; lag phases 14–26 days. Hermes called it methane, a "~2 t/day pilot" at a Moscow institute, and gave a fake DOI.
- **Soviet and Russian history — Nevzorova 2020** (T. Nevzorova, KTH, "Biogas Production in the Russian Federation: Current Status, Potential, and Barriers", Energies 13:3620, https://doi.org/10.3390/en13143620, open access): methane fermentation studied since the 1940s; "The industrial production and use of biogas began in the 1960s. Even then, energy was derived from municipal, forest, and agricultural waste." Kalyuzhnyi 2008 adds about 60 years of thermophilic sludge digestion, used almost only at Moscow treatment plants. No Soviet publication on yard-waste AD was found (NOT FOUND).
- **Plants (Nevzorova 2020, Table A1)**: JSC Sharkansky RTP, Sharkan (Udmurt Republic), 2011, 300 m3 biogas/day, feedstock "any organic waste: grass, silage, and slaughterhouse waste" (EnergoRezhim) — the only plant listing grass. Moscow Kuryanovo (128,000 m3 biogas/day) and Lyubertsy (2012, 145,000 m3/day) treat sewage sludge. Luchki, Belgorod (2012, 2.4 MW) takes meat-processing waste, pig slurry and corn silage. No green-waste plant found.
- **Rules**: GOST R 70718-2023 (in force 1 Jun 2023, voluntary) — methodological recommendations for utilising organic fractions of municipal solid waste "with composting methods". It covers organic MSW fractions, plant waste belonging to MSW (FKKO code 73130000000) and kitchen/catering waste; §6.6 describes AD briefly (example: 10–16 days at 25 °C, biogas about 65% methane; dry systems 30–40% dry matter, wet systems 10–25%). Open copy: https://files.stroyinf.ru/Data/799/79993.pdf. The Russian Environmental Operator's carbon-project methodology "Anaerobic digestion of organic waste" (version 1, 6 Mar 2024; https://carbonreg.ru/pdf/methodology/) applies to AD of organic waste including MSW organics, food-soiled paper, agro-industrial waste and industrial wastewater, and excludes manure.
- **Research groups confirmed by opened sources**: Winogradsky Institute of Microbiology, RAS — monograph "Biotechnology and microbiology of anaerobic processing of organic municipal waste" (eds. A.N. Nozhevnikova, A.Yu. Kallistova, Yu.V. Litti, M.V. Kevbrina; Universitetskaya Kniga, 2020 edition, ISBN 978-5-98699-166-5; covers MSW and sewage sludge; paywalled, catalogue page https://znanium.ru/catalog/document?id=367346). Kazan Scientific Centre RAS (Karaeva). Mendeleev University of Chemical Technology, Moscow (Bragazina & Ermolenko 2023, review of plant-waste uses incl. biogas, https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/analiz-vozmozhnyh-napravleniy-ispolzovaniya-rastitelnyh-othodov-selskogo-hozyaystva-i-pischevoy-promyshlennosti). Pacific National University, Khabarovsk (Shevtsov et al. 2023, sewage-sludge digestion only, https://doi.org/10.24866/2227-6858/2023-3/90-104).
