## Summary

No source opened in this slice describes an operating dedicated yard- or garden-waste anaerobic digestion plant in Bosnia and Herzegovina. What was opened is more limited: a Federation of BiH waste plan that sets operational objectives for separately collecting garden and park bio-waste and for using biodegradable waste to make compost and biofuels; a Canton Sarajevo waste plan that gives separate-collection targets for biodegradable waste from gardens and parks and, in a later section, estimates biodegradable kitchen and garden waste and says that, because biodegradable waste is not collected separately, most of it goes to the Smiljevići landfill; a regional bio-waste guideline prepared for, among other cities, Bijeljina in Republika Srpska, which lists green (garden) waste among acceptable anaerobic-digestion feedstocks; and a University of Tuzla laboratory paper on anaerobic co-digestion of sewage sludge with kitchen waste and fruit-and-vegetable processing waste (not dedicated yard-waste AD). This slice does not cover the full legal, plant, yield, or simulator inventory that the complete country survey would require.

A second bite-size slice the same day still found no operating dedicated yard- or garden-waste AD plant. The official March 2026 draft of Book 2 of the FBiH waste plan reports 2023 separate-collection figures for biodegradable waste, sets a 2032 separate-collection target, and analyzes future biogas plants that would treat household biodegradable waste and waste from green areas, with a feasibility study for such a plant in preparation in West Herzegovina Canton. Official eJN notices show that KJKP RAD Sarajevo opened a March 2026 procedure for a feasibility study and conceptual design of a composting plant and a biogas plant at the Smiljevići landfill for Canton Sarajevo bio-waste, and that that procedure was cancelled on 8 May 2026. A 14 May 2026 news report describes a later tender and attributes capacity figures to tender documents that were not opened here (yard waste to composting; kitchen waste to a planned biogas unit). A 2023 University of Tuzla paper used the IWA Anaerobic Digestion Model No. 1 (ADM1) to simulate co-digestion of municipal sludge with fruit and vegetable waste; that is not a yard-waste yield and not a named software package.
