## Summary

No Vietnamese digester taking yard, garden or park waste was found. Vietnam's biogas sector is household-scale: "2003 - 2020: 290,337 biodigesters by 6 different Biogas Programs" (SNV), almost all on livestock manure. The most relevant measured number is a lab BMP at Hanoi University of Agriculture: lawn grass gave 220 NL CH4/kg VS, duckweed 340 and water spinach 110.6 (Cu et al. 2015). In the Mekong Delta, Can Tho University and JIRCAS fed cut plants into household digesters: grass weed and duckweed gave 90 % and water hyacinth 70 % of the biogas of pig manure. Rice straw and water hyacinth work as co-substrates after simple soaking pretreatment. Vietnam's environmental decree defines domestic solid waste but has no green-waste category and no digestion rule for it.

## Verified findings

- **Lawn grass BMP (Hanoi)** — Cu, Nguyen, Triolo, Pedersen, Le, Le & Sommer, Asian-Australasian Journal of Animal Sciences 28(2) 280–289, 2015, doi 10.5713/ajas.14.0312 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4283175/, open, CC BY-NC): "Methane production from duckweed (Spirodela polyrrhiza) was higher than from lawn grass and water spinach at 340, 220, and 110.6 NL CH4 kg−1 VS, respectively." Table 2: grass 220.53 (SD 30.91), 16.9 % DM, 6.35 % lignin. Piglet manure 443. Tested at the Hanoi University of Agriculture lab with the University of Southern Denmark. Caution: one results paragraph of the paper swaps the three plant values; the abstract, table and conclusion agree.
- **Cut plants in household digesters (Can Tho)** — JIRCAS Research Highlight 2014 (Izumi, Matsubara; https://www.jircas.go.jp/en/publication/research_results/2014_a01): 2.7 kg DM/day of plants cut to 20–30 cm for 30 days; "biogas production from water hyacinth and from both duckweed and grass weed was 70% and 90%, respectively, compared to the control" (pig manure). One household on Pistia only for a year replaced 2.4 t of fuel wood. Of 435 monitored households, 44 stopped using biogas for more than a day, 55 % because they had no pigs.
- **Can Tho University (CENRes) programme** — Trần Sỹ Nam, Huỳnh Văn Thảo et al., Tạp chí Khoa học ĐH Cần Thơ 58 (SDMD) 239–251, 2022, doi 10.22144/ctu.jvn.2022.210 (https://ctujsvn.ctu.edu.vn/index.php/ctujsvn/article/view/4841): 515 PE digesters with a carbon credit of 446 tCO2/yr, 32 improved HDPE bag digesters (~7.64 m3) designed to take straw, water hyacinth or other plants; straw soaked in biogas effluent or pond-sludge water and mixed 50:50 with pig manure gave 78–84 % more biogas than manure alone, and pretreated water hyacinth 80–107 % more. With shrimp-pond sludge (C/N 25), rice straw gave 104.3 L biogas and 46.25 L CH4 per kg VS (source: "104,3" and "46,25", Vietnamese decimal commas). The community biogas-sharing model cut 12.9 t CO2eq/yr.
- **Rice straw and water hyacinth batch tests** (checker find) — Trần Sỹ Nam et al. 2017, doi 10.22144/ctu.jsi.2017.035: rice straw at 15–20 gVS/L gave the most biogas, 66–70.4 mL/g VS ("66 – 70,4"); water hyacinth 23–23.4 mL/g VS at 2–2.5 gVS/L.
- **Food waste + water hyacinth** (checker find) — Nguyễn Công Thuận & Trần Sỹ Nam 2023, doi 10.22144/ctu.jvn.2023.101: biogas yield rose from 1.08 L/(kg TS·day) for food waste alone to 5.01 for water hyacinth alone; CH4 28.25 → 44.51 %.
- **Spent straw-mushroom compost** — Nguyen Vo Chau Ngan & Klaus Fricke, J. Vietnamese Environment 3(1) 2012, doi 10.13141/jve.vol3.no1.pp4-9: up to 75 % of the mix with pig manure gave biogas not significantly different from manure alone; "methane contents … around 60%".
- **National programmes** — SNV slides (VEPG 2021): 290,337 household digesters 2003–2020 from six programmes; the SNV-MARD programme built 181,683 in 53 provinces; 3,072,265 Gold Standard VERs issued. ADB LCASP (Loan 2968-VIE, EMR Jul–Dec 2017): 3,730 small biogas plants built in the period, 47,948 in total in 10 provinces; target "At least 70% bio-slurry is converted to organic fertilizers".
- **Law** — Decree 08/2022/NĐ-CP (10 Jan 2022), Art. 3(11): "Chất thải rắn sinh hoạt (còn gọi là rác thải sinh hoạt) là chất thải rắn phát sinh trong sinh hoạt thường ngày của con người." No green-waste category and no digestion requirement found.
- **An Giang household pilot** — Tran Thi Hieu, Le Thanh Hai et al. (VNU-HCM), 2020, doi 10.32508/stdjsee.v4i2.523: the mango garden's leaves and dry branches are burned to biochar that filters biogas effluent (COD 2,932 → 375 mg/L); the digester takes pig manure, not garden waste.
