## Summary

France has a large green-waste resource (about 61 Mt/yr gross, 28% of it lawn clippings, per a 2015 FranceAgriMer study), but official and sector sources describe green waste as a poor stand-alone AD feedstock: woody fractions are steered to composting, and grass is only a small co-substrate in agricultural or territorial co-digestion plants. Verified research on lignocellulosic AD is lab or pilot scale (INRAE LBE Narbonne leach-bed solid-state AD of wheat straw; IMT Atlantique / CSTB 30 L co-digestion with ensiled green waste). Since 1 January 2024, source sorting of biowaste (which legally includes garden and park waste) is generalised under the AGEC law. No French full-scale plant digesting mainly yard waste was found.

## Verified findings

- **FranceAgriMer, "Valorisation des déchets verts en France"** (Synthèses FranceAgriMer, Biomasse no. 3, May 2015; study by E&E Consultant and SOLAGRO, Dec 2014; https://www.franceagrimer.fr/sites/default/files/rdd/documents/Cahier_Les%20d%C3%A9chets%20verts_2.pdf). Gross green-waste resource 61,358,691 t: about 60% prunings, 28% lawn clippings, 6% leaves, 6% tree work. AD is held back by high capital cost and the "limited methanogenic potential of green waste"; seasonal variability hurts AD more than composting; lawn clippings should be stored only 2–3 days (at most a week).
- **ADEME technical sheet "Méthanisation"** (Feb 2014; https://www.methafrance.fr/sites/default/files/2021-06/fiche_technique_methanisation-ademe.pdf). Lists lawn clippings among municipal AD substrates; woody waste is poorly suited to AD and should be composted; adding biowaste and green/horticultural waste lowers N, P and K in manure-based digestates; biomethane sold at 64–125 €/MWh under the tariff of that time.
- **INRAE LBE Narbonne, solid-state AD of wheat straw** (Rouches E., Escudié R., Latrille E., Carrère H., "Solid-state anaerobic digestion of wheat straw: Impact of S/I ratio and pilot-scale fungal pretreatment", Waste Management 85:464–476, 2019, doi:10.1016/j.wasman.2019.01.006; open copy https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-02623452v1). Leach-bed reactor; start-up worked at S/I 1.2–3.6 (VS basis); reactors recovered from acidification when VFA/alkalinity stayed below about 2 gHAc_eq/gCaCO3, so the usual wet-AD limit of 0.6 does not apply to solid-state AD. Fungal-pretreated straw gave 254 vs 215 NmL CH4/g VS for the control, but after counting pretreatment mass loss 161 vs 171 NmL/g initial TS (60 days). Pretreatment mainly helped start-up.
- **IMT Atlantique (GEPEA) and CSTB, Nantes, urban small-scale AD** (Bautista Angeli J.R., LeFloc'h T., Lakel A., Lacarrière B., Andres Y., "Anaerobic digestion of urban wastes: integration and benefits of a small-scale system", Environmental Technology 43(22):3414–3425, 2022, epub 2021, doi:10.1080/09593330.2021.1921857; PubMed 33890842, abstract open). Settled wastewater, dried food waste and **ensiled green waste** co-digested in a 30 L reactor: 83% VS reduction, stable 321 mL CH4/g VS added over 35 days. A neighbourhood model found 14% of the annual green waste could be processed daily; heat output was about 15 times electricity. (The draft wrongly tied this to the DECISIVE pilot and INRAE Rennes.)
- **DECISIVE micro-AD pilot, Ecully (Lyon)** (INRAE press release, 20 Oct 2021, https://www.inrae.fr/en/news/valorizing-urban-biowaste-locally-conclusive-results-european-decisive-project; H2020 grant 689229, coordinated by INRAE, Anne Trémier). From Nov 2019; designed for at least 50 t/yr of biowaste, mainly restaurant and catering waste; sized for 20 m3 biogas/day, achieved 15 m3/day; 80% of biogas for heat, 20% for electricity; INRAE Rennes also developed a two-phase leach-bed micro-AD process. Green waste is not named as a feedstock.
- **Chantonnay Pierre-Brune biomethane plant (Vendée), ENGIE BiOZ** (https://bioz-biomethane.com/nos-centrales/centrale-biogaz-de-chantonnay-pierre-brune/). Commissioned Sep 2018; about 30,000 t/yr: 65% agricultural, 30% agri-food, 5% local-authority waste (school canteen waste, and the plant "can also" take lawn clippings); 6,000 m3 in two digesters, retention over 50 days; about 2.15 million m3/yr, about 22 GWh/yr; 250 Nm3/h injected. The draft's "200,000 Nm3/yr" repeats a typo in the operator's 2020 project sheet.
- **SUEZ Digelis FoodWaste** (SUEZ press release, 2026, https://www.suez.com/en/news/press-releases/suez-launches-digelis-foodwaste). Pretreatment demonstrator at the Meta-Bio-Energies AD site, Ombrée d'Anjou, since Jan 2026, up to 2,000 t/yr; depackaging, 55 °C biological step, mechanical separation, then conventional AD. Company claims: up to +5% biogas, up to −40% digestate volume, no added water. This is a **food-waste** process; green-waste use is not stated.
- **BioBéarn, Mourenx, TotalEnergies** (https://totalenergies.com/company/projects/biomass-biogas/biobearn-methanization-unit-france). Since Jan 2023; 160 GWh/yr capacity; 220,000 t/yr from farming and agri-food; about 200,000 t/yr digestate spread within 50 km, saving about 5,000 t of chemical fertiliser. **No green waste is stated** (out of scope except as a scale reference).

## Policy

- **Biowaste source sorting**: generalised from 1 Jan 2024 under the AGEC law (loi n° 2020-105 of 10 Feb 2020). Legally, biowaste includes garden and park waste (lawn clippings, leaves, hedge trimmings). Local authorities must offer a sorting solution: home or shared composting, separate collection, or drop-off points (https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/biodechets; https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/actualites/A16963).
- **Permitting (ICPE rubrique 2781)**: 2781-1 covers raw plant matter, manure and similar inputs: under 30 t/day is declaration, 30–100 t/day is registration, 100 t/day or more is authorisation. 2781-2 covers other non-hazardous waste: registration under 100 t/day, authorisation at 100 t/day or more (decree of 6 Jun 2018). A regional energy syndicate's synthesis (SDE35) lists green waste (lawn, roadside mowing) under 2781-1 (https://aida.ineris.fr/reglementation/2781-installation-methanisation-dechets-non-dangereux-matiere-vegetale-brute-a). A yard-waste-only digester would therefore fall in the lighter 2781-1 class; adding food waste moves it into 2781-2.

## Digestate

The ADEME 2014 sheet says green waste dilutes the nutrients in digestate, and recommends composting to mature the digestate. BioBéarn spreads its digestate on farmland within 50 km. No French nutrient-recovery (struvite or ammonia stripping) plant for green-waste digestate was verified.

## Simulators and modeling tools

Simulators found (after check):

- **DIGES 3**: developer ADEME with EVEA (earlier versions DIGES 1/2 by Cemagref, now INRAE). Calculates the greenhouse-gas, energy and nitrogen balance of a digestion or co-digestion unit. Not a kinetic process model. Free download from ADEME; licence terms not stated. https://librairie.ademe.fr/changement-climatique/6776-outil-diges-3-evaluation-environnementale-d-une-unite-de-methanisation.html (online 19 Jan 2024). French use: ADEME 2026 study applying DIGES 3 to 100 French AD units (SOLAGRO / AAMF / AILE). DIGES 2 guide: Bioteau & Dabert, Cemagref 2009, HAL hal-02593780.
- **SUMO**: developer Dynamita, headquartered in France. A wastewater and resource-recovery process simulator that includes anaerobic digestion and solids treatment. Commercial (annual lease, trial licences on request). https://dynamita.com/. This is a checker find, not from the Hermes draft; no yard-waste use was found.

Not taken: the draft's "ADM2" (HAL 02593780 is the DIGES 2 GHG guide, not an ADM2 model); "IMT Atlantique ADM1 MATLAB" (HAL 01086039 is a Spanish sludge-ADM1 paper); "METHAVALORG toolbox" (an APESA R&D programme, not a tool). ADM1F (US) and BioWin (Canada) are real but already covered in earlier country notes, and no French use was confirmed. SimaPro is a real LCA tool, but the claimed ADEME green-waste LCA was not found. Nothing was installed.
