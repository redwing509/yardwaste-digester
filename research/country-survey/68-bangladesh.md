## Summary

Bangladesh has no commercial or municipal digester fed with yard, park or street-tree waste. The only measured yard-waste digestion is lab-scale work on fallen leaves in 2-litre bottles at BCSIR (Dhaka) and the Islamic University of Technology (Gazipur). Towns do run a few digesters for mixed or kitchen organic waste: Jashore since 2019, an 80 m³ plant in Gaibandha, a small one in Faridpur, and one at the Narayanganj compost plant. At Jashore, grass and leaves go to composting, not to the digester. Dhaka's organic waste is mostly composted (Waste Concern) or landfilled. Dhaka South signed a mixed-waste resource-recovery pilot at Matuail in December 2025 that lists biogas among its outputs. Dhaka's waste has been put at 11 % garden waste by weight, but no tonnage figure was found. The 2010 National 3R Strategy asks city corporations to bring in private firms for biogas or compost plants. No biomethane rule or incentive was found.

## Verified findings

- **Fallen-leaf digestion (BCSIR, lab)** — Rouf, Islam, Rabeya & Mondal (IFRD, BCSIR), Bangladesh J. Sci. Ind. Res. 50(3):163–168 (2015): mixed campus leaves (mahogany 75 %, eucalyptus 10 %, rain tree 15 %), 2-litre bottles, 60 days, ambient. "The biogas reactor containing 6% crushed and pretreated leaves mixed with 2% cow dung (on 8% TS basis) gave maximum (0.199 l/g) biogas yield", against 0.0106 l/g for untreated leaves; CH₄ 69.3 %; VS reduction 35.33 %, COD reduction 34.89 %; leaves C/N 40:1. https://doi.org/10.3329/bjsir.v50i3.25579
- **Fallen-leaf co-digestion (IUT, lab)** — Khaled, Kamal, Porosh, Mashfy & Rahman (Islamic University of Technology), BIO Web Conf. 62, 03003 (2023): jackfruit, mango, mahogany and teak leaves, 1 % (w/v) cow dung, urea for pH, 2-litre bottles. "Teak leaves had the highest methane yield at 64.3%, followed by Jackfruit leaves at 62.2%". These are methane shares in the gas, not yields per tonne. https://doi.org/10.1051/bioconf/20236203003
- **Grass and flower clippings in co-digestion (IDCOL/BAU)** — BSERT, Bangladesh Agricultural University, for IDCOL (2018): "Soft organics refer to weeds and grass, flower clipping and non-processed food waste". At 5 % with cow manure, "methane concentrations ranged 59– 75%, in average the biogas yield was 13% higher and for CH4, 24% more yield"; above 10 %, digestion was unstable. https://idcol.org/download/Research%20and%20Development%20on%20Biogas%20Production%20Efficiency%20in%20Domestic%20Bio....pdf
- **Municipal organic-waste digesters (not yard waste)** — Jashore Municipality centre (since August 2019, Tk26.33 crore): "The centre can produce 720 cubic metres of biogas per day, but it is currently producing 450 cubic metres"; grass and leaves go to the compost plant and "The rest goes to the biogas plant" (https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/industry/putting-trash-use-jashore-waste-management-centre-paves-way-194935). Gaibandha's 80 m³ kitchen-waste plant (2012) "treats 800-1000 kg wastes/day", and Faridpur has had a 6.8 m³ plant since 2009 (https://www.ctc-n.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/PPP%20led%20Community%20based%20urban%20waste%20to%20biogas%20.pdf). The Narayanganj compost plant (22 t/day, 2016) has "1 Biogas plant" (https://ccet.jp/sites/default/files/inline-files/New%20Presentation%20of%20Programmatic%20CDM.pdf).
- **Dhaka composting and the Matuail pilot** — Waste Concern runs 5 community compost plants (10–12, 3, 3, 1 and 1 t/day), and the C40 page says "they are building 700 tons/day-capacity compost plants" (https://www.c40.org/case-studies/c40-good-practice-guides-dhaka-composting-project/). DSCC and B&F (Korea), 4 Dec 2025: mixed waste to "organic fertiliser, biogas, refuse-derived fuel (RDF), biofuel", ramping "from 300 metric tons of waste per day during the pilot phase to about 3,500 metric tons per day". No digester design or yard-waste feed is stated (https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/environment/dhaka-south-teams-korean-firm-turn-trash-energy-and-fertilizer-1302071).
- **Market-waste digester design (Dhaka, not built)** — van Groenestijn & Voogt, Wageningen Food & Biobased Research report 2436 (2023): a 100 t/day central digester for fresh-market waste, "estimated at 103 million BDT", "payback time ... 3.4 years". https://doi.org/10.18174/632787
- **Garden-waste share** — the DoE / Waste Concern / ITN-BUET SAARC country paper (2004), Table "Composition of Waste of Dhaka City": "Garden Waste 11" (% by weight, citing Ahmed & Rahman 2000). https://wasteconcern.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/0b2a706a-ec46-4ed3-a864-c48ff25ac72a.pdf
- **HTC and AD+HTC (BUET, not yard waste)** — HTC of municipal solid organic waste from the Amin Bazar landfill (74.2 % food waste): optimum "275◦C, with a residence time of 20 minutes and a water loading of 90%", "biocoal retained 82.7% of energy" (Aurnob, MSc thesis 2019, http://lib.buet.ac.bd:8080/xmlui/handle/123456789/5424). An LCA of AD with HTC of the digestate found HTC process water to be "a key pollutant" (Hossain, MSc thesis 2023, http://lib.buet.ac.bd:8080/xmlui/handle/123456789/6618).
- **Policy** — National 3R Strategy (DoE, December 2010): "The City Corporations/Pourashavas will engage private sector to establish recycling plants for organic waste such as biogas or composting plant." No tariff, biomethane rule or taka incentive is attached. https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/bgd190976.pdf

## Simulators and modeling tools

Simulators found: openLCA (GreenDelta; free, source code MPL-2.0; https://www.openlca.org/) was used for the BUET AD+HTC life-cycle assessment ("the impact modeling was done with OpenLCA 1.10.3"). It is an LCA tool, not a digester process simulator. It was added to the candidate list only and nothing was installed. These were also mentioned but not added: MATLAB/Simulink and US EPA LandGEM (both in the IUBAT biogas-potential paper; LandGEM is a landfill-gas model), and a web "Anaerobic Digestion Calculator" (biogasworld.com, link now broken). No Bangladeshi-developed digester simulator was found.
